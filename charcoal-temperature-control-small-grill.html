Temperature Control on a Small Open Charcoal Grill

The Slot Grill Compact is an open charcoal grill: no lid, no dampers, no dials. That sounds like less control, but it's actually simpler. You control heat with three things you can see: how much charcoal is lit, where you put it, and where you put the food. Learn those and you can run anything from a hard steak sear to gentle eggs on the griddle.

The three levers

LeverMore heatLess heat
Amount of lit fuelMore coals, piled deeperFewer coals, single layer
Coal placementPile under the foodMove coals away; leave an empty zone
Food placementOver the deep coalsOver the thin or empty zone

On the Compact the food sits close to the coals, so these levers act fast. Moving a steak four inches toward the cool end changes its heat more than you'd expect.

Reading the heat: the hand test

Hold your palm flat about where the food will sit, over the zone you want to check, and count how long you can comfortably keep it there.

SecondsHeat levelGood for
About 2HighSearing steaks, smash burgers, skirt steak, blistering peppers
4–5MediumBurgers, sausages, pork chops, shrimp, most vegetables
7+LowFinishing chicken, holding food, warming tortillas, eggs on the griddle

It's approximate. Use it to pick a zone, and use an instant-read thermometer to decide when food is done.

Setting heat for common cooks

  • High and fast (steak, skirt, smash burgers): a fuller chimney, coals piled under most of the grate, a small cool zone for rescue.
  • Medium two-zone (burgers, chops, sausages, wings): coals across two-thirds, last third empty.
  • Low and gentle (bone-in chicken start, fish on the griddle, eggs): a single layer of coals, or food kept on the cool end.
  • Griddle: the griddle plate spreads heat. Put more coals under the area you want hot and fewer where you want to hold food.

Turning heat up mid-cook

  1. Push coals together with tongs. Spread-out coals lose heat quickly.
  2. Knock ash off the coals by tapping the side of a coal with tongs; ash insulates.
  3. Add a handful of fresh charcoal to the hot side. Lump catches faster than briquettes.
  4. For big boosts, pour in lit coals from a second chimney instead of cold ones.

Turning heat down mid-cook

  1. Move food to the cool side first. It's instant.
  2. Spread coals out or push some toward the far end.
  3. Lift the food off the heat for a minute onto a clean tray.
  4. Don't spray water on coals to cool them; it throws ash and steam onto the food.

Weather changes everything

  • Wind feeds the coals and makes them burn hotter and faster on the windward side. Point the hot end into the wind, or shelter the grill with safe clearance.
  • Cold pulls heat from the steel and the food. Start with more fuel.
  • Rain or damp slows lighting. Keep fuel dry and allow extra time.

Keeping a steady fire for longer cooks

For anything over about 30–40 minutes, plan to feed the fire. Add a few coals to the hot side every 20–30 minutes so new fuel catches before the old fuel fades. Clear ash from the previous cook before you light; ash in the bottom of the Compact's firebox restricts the airflow that keeps coals burning. Between cooks, once ash is cold, you can pull one side panel, empty it, and slide the panel back in.

Why the Compact's design helps

  • Thick steel (12-gauge carbon steel) holds and evens out heat better than thin sheet metal.
  • Air gap: 2⅞" between the table and the bottom of the coal bed helps minimize radiant heat to the surface below.
  • Two-piece top: run a grate and a griddle side by side and use each over a different heat zone.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix

Hot and cold spots on the griddleSpread coals more evenly under it, or use the spots on purpose

Safe internal temperatures

Guessing is how small-grill cooks go wrong, because everything happens fast over a close fire. Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from bone. USDA minimum internal temperatures:

FoodMinimum internal temp
Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts145°F, then rest 3 minutes
Ground beef, pork, veal and lamb160°F
Poultry (whole, parts, ground)165°F
Fish and shellfish145°F, or flesh opaque and separates easily with a fork
Egg dishes160°F; cook eggs until yolk and white are firm
Leftovers and casseroles165°F

Quick-start checklist

  • Grill seasoned and dry; old ash emptied
  • Stable, level, non-combustible spot with clearance
  • Charcoal lit in a chimney, ashed over
  • Two-zone fire built, grates preheated and oiled
  • Thermometer, tongs, spatula and gloves within reach
  • Clean tray ready for cooked food

Common small-grill mistakes (and the fix)

  1. Cooking before the coals are ready. Black-topped coals give smoky, weak heat and sticking. Wait until they glow under a light coat of gray ash.
  2. Wall-to-wall coals. With no cool zone, every flare-up becomes a burn. Leave at least a third of the firebox thin or empty.
  3. Crowding the grate. 146 sq in fills fast. Leave space between pieces so you can move them, and cook in waves.
  4. Moving food too early. Let it sear and release on its own; it sticks until a crust forms.
  5. Cold, dirty grates. Preheat for a few minutes, scrape, and wipe with an oiled paper towel held in tongs.
  6. Wet food. Pat meat and vegetables dry before oiling and seasoning. Moisture steams instead of searing.
  7. Judging doneness by time or color. Small fires vary. Use a thermometer.
  8. Leaving ash in the firebox. Old ash chokes airflow. Empty it once cold before the next cook.
  9. Putting the grill away wet. Raw carbon steel needs to be dried and lightly oiled. See rust prevention.

After the cook: burn-down and pack-down

  1. Let the remaining coals burn down. Don't leave a fire unattended.
  2. When ash and steel are completely cold, dump the ash into a metal container with a lid, or dispose of it where the venue or campground allows.
  3. Scrape the grates, then wipe the cooking surfaces with a light coat of oil or Slot Grill Seasoning Balm.
  4. Lift off the grates, slide out the V side panels, pull the end pieces and stack. The Compact packs to about 1" thick.
  5. Store it dry. A bag or old box keeps soot off the rest of your gear.

Safety basics (every cook)

  • Outdoors only. Never use a charcoal grill indoors, in a garage, tent, camper, boat cabin or vehicle. Burning charcoal makes carbon monoxide, which you cannot see or smell.
  • Stable, level, non-combustible surface. Bare soil, gravel, sand, concrete, pavers or a fire-safe pad. The Compact's 2⅞" air gap between the table and the bottom of the coal bed helps minimize radiant heat to the surface below, but it is still a live fire.
  • Clearance. Keep it away from tents, vehicles, siding, overhangs, dry grass and anything that can burn. Keep kids and pets back.
  • Follow local rules. Burn bans, campground, park, venue and HOA rules come first.
  • Water or a fire extinguisher nearby, plus heat-safe gloves for handling grates and panels.
  • Ash stays hot for a long time. Put it in a metal container with a lid and never pack hot steel or ash into a vehicle.

Frequently asked questions

How do you control temperature on a grill with no lid or vents?

By the amount of lit charcoal, where you place the coals, and where you place the food. A two-zone fire gives you a hot side and a cool side to move food between.

What is the hand test for grill heat?

Hold your palm where the food will cook. About 2 seconds before you need to pull away is high heat, 4 to 5 seconds is medium, and 7 or more is low. It is approximate; use a thermometer for doneness.

How do I make the fire hotter quickly?

Push the coals together, knock off the ash, and add lit coals from a second chimney or a handful of lump to the hot side.

Should I spray water on coals to cool them down?

No. Move the food to the cool side instead. Water kicks ash and steam onto the food.
