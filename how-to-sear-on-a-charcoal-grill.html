How to Get a Hard Sear on a Small Charcoal Grill

A good sear is the reason most people cook over charcoal in the first place: a deep brown crust, real grill marks, and flavor you can't get from a pan on a weak stove. Small charcoal grills are actually excellent searing tools, because the food sits close to the coals and the 12-gauge carbon steel of the Slot Grill Compact holds heat. Here's how to get it right every time.

What a sear actually is

Browning happens when the surface of food gets hot and dry enough for sugars and proteins to react, well above the boiling point of water. Anything that keeps the surface wet or cool (moisture, a cold grate, weak coals, a crowded grate) delays browning, and the food overcooks inside while you wait. Every tip below is about getting the surface hot and dry, fast.

Step by step

  1. Dry the food. Pat steaks, chops, burgers and vegetables dry with paper towels. For steak, salting 40 minutes or more ahead (or overnight in the fridge, uncovered) draws moisture out and then lets it reabsorb, leaving a drier surface. If you can't wait 40 minutes, salt right before cooking.
  2. Oil the food, not just the grate. A thin film of high-smoke-point oil helps contact and heat transfer.
  3. Build a deep hot zone. Pile coals two layers deep under the sear area in a two-zone fire. Lump charcoal burns hotter for this job; briquettes work too with a fuller chimney.
  4. Wait for ready coals. Glowing under gray ash. Check with the hand test: about 2 seconds is high heat.
  5. Preheat the grates for several minutes over the coals. Hot steel gives marks and releases food.
  6. Scrape and oil the grates with an oiled paper towel in tongs.
  7. Lay the food down and leave it. It will stick at first. When the crust forms, it releases.
  8. Flip once the crust is set, usually a couple of minutes for a steak over hot coals. For thick cuts, flip more often after the first crust for more even cooking inside.
  9. Finish on the cool side if the inside needs more time. Check with an instant-read thermometer.
  10. Rest steaks and chops a few minutes before slicing.

Grill marks vs. full crust

Grill marks are stripes of deep browning where the grate touches. A full crust is browning across the whole surface. On a grate, you get marks plus good browning between them from radiant heat. For an edge-to-edge crust, sear on a Slot Grill griddle (plancha) instead: it's the same thick carbon steel as the grill and gives full contact, like a cast-iron pan over live fire. The Compact is strong enough to support cast iron, so a skillet works too.

Searing on the griddle plate

  • Put the griddle over the deeper coals and let it heat thoroughly. A drop of water should skitter and vanish.
  • Add a thin layer of oil just before the food.
  • Press thin items (smash burgers, skirt steak) flat for full contact.
  • Keep the griddle seasoned so food releases; see seasoning.

Safe internal temperatures

Guessing is how small-grill cooks go wrong, because everything happens fast over a close fire. Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from bone. USDA minimum internal temperatures:

FoodMinimum internal temp
Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts145°F, then rest 3 minutes
Ground beef, pork, veal and lamb160°F
Poultry (whole, parts, ground)165°F
Fish and shellfish145°F, or flesh opaque and separates easily with a fork
Egg dishes160°F; cook eggs until yolk and white are firm
Leftovers and casseroles165°F

Quick-start checklist

  • Grill seasoned and dry; old ash emptied
  • Stable, level, non-combustible spot with clearance
  • Charcoal lit in a chimney, ashed over
  • Two-zone fire built, grates preheated and oiled
  • Thermometer, tongs, spatula and gloves within reach
  • Clean tray ready for cooked food

Common small-grill mistakes (and the fix)

  1. Cooking before the coals are ready. Black-topped coals give smoky, weak heat and sticking. Wait until they glow under a light coat of gray ash.
  2. Wall-to-wall coals. With no cool zone, every flare-up becomes a burn. Leave at least a third of the firebox thin or empty.
  3. Crowding the grate. 146 sq in fills fast. Leave space between pieces so you can move them, and cook in waves.
  4. Moving food too early. Let it sear and release on its own; it sticks until a crust forms.
  5. Cold, dirty grates. Preheat for a few minutes, scrape, and wipe with an oiled paper towel held in tongs.
  6. Wet food. Pat meat and vegetables dry before oiling and seasoning. Moisture steams instead of searing.
  7. Judging doneness by time or color. Small fires vary. Use a thermometer.
  8. Leaving ash in the firebox. Old ash chokes airflow. Empty it once cold before the next cook.
  9. Putting the grill away wet. Raw carbon steel needs to be dried and lightly oiled. See rust prevention.

After the cook: burn-down and pack-down

  1. Let the remaining coals burn down. Don't leave a fire unattended.
  2. When ash and steel are completely cold, dump the ash into a metal container with a lid, or dispose of it where the venue or campground allows.
  3. Scrape the grates, then wipe the cooking surfaces with a light coat of oil or Slot Grill Seasoning Balm.
  4. Lift off the grates, slide out the V side panels, pull the end pieces and stack. The Compact packs to about 1" thick.
  5. Store it dry. A bag or old box keeps soot off the rest of your gear.

Safety basics (every cook)

  • Outdoors only. Never use a charcoal grill indoors, in a garage, tent, camper, boat cabin or vehicle. Burning charcoal makes carbon monoxide, which you cannot see or smell.
  • Stable, level, non-combustible surface. Bare soil, gravel, sand, concrete, pavers or a fire-safe pad. The Compact's 2⅞" air gap between the table and the bottom of the coal bed helps minimize radiant heat to the surface below, but it is still a live fire.
  • Clearance. Keep it away from tents, vehicles, siding, overhangs, dry grass and anything that can burn. Keep kids and pets back.
  • Follow local rules. Burn bans, campground, park, venue and HOA rules come first.
  • Water or a fire extinguisher nearby, plus heat-safe gloves for handling grates and panels.
  • Ash stays hot for a long time. Put it in a metal container with a lid and never pack hot steel or ash into a vehicle.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get a good sear on a small charcoal grill?

Dry the food, oil it lightly, pile coals deep under one side, preheat the grates, and leave the food alone until it releases. Finish thick cuts on the cool side.

Is lump charcoal better for searing?

Lump generally lights faster and burns hotter, which many cooks prefer for searing. Briquettes also work with a fuller chimney.

Can I use a cast-iron skillet on the Slot Grill Compact?

Yes. Slot Grill describes the Compact as strong enough to support cast iron. A Slot Grill griddle (plancha) is another full-contact option.

When should I flip a steak?

When the crust has formed and the steak releases from the grate on its own. For thick steaks, flipping more often after the first crust gives more even cooking.
