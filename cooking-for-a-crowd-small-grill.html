Cooking for a Crowd on a Small Charcoal Grill

The Slot Grill Compact is sized for one to three people. But owners take it to group camping trips, tailgates and job sites all the time, and it can feed more people than you'd think if you plan for it. The secret is to stop thinking about one big batch and start thinking in waves.

"fantastic grill!!! Went with a group of people and everybody loved using it!!!"Cheryl Norris, Compact owner

The math of a small grate

The cooking area is 16.25" × 9" (146 sq in). How many pieces fit depends on size and how much space you leave to move things, so count at home: lay your burgers, sausages or chicken on a sheet pan in a 16" × 9" rectangle. That's one wave. Then plan how many waves you need and how long each takes.

Pick a crowd-friendly menu

  • Foods that hold well after cooking: sausages, brats, chicken thighs, wings, pulled or sliced meats, sausage and peppers.
  • Foods that cook fast: smash burgers, shrimp, skewers, thin steak.
  • Foods that are better shared: fajitas and tacos stretch one steak across many people.
  • Sides that don't need the grill: slaw, beans, chips, salads, fruit. Save grill space for protein.
  • Avoid anything that needs a long, slow cook and a lot of space unless you have time.

Plan the waves

  1. Wave 1, holders: sausages or chicken. They're forgiving and hold in a covered pan.
  2. Wave 2, the main event: burgers or steak while people are ready to eat.
  3. Wave 3, fast extras: peppers and onions on the griddle, shrimp, vegetables.
  4. Wave 4, dessert: s'mores and grilled fruit on the dying coals.

Keep the fire going

  • Start with a full chimney.
  • Light a second chimney about 20–30 minutes in, on a safe non-combustible surface, so you have lit coals ready to add.
  • Keep the two-zone layout: the cool end is your holding shelf.
  • Push coals together and add fuel to the hot side; see temperature control.

Holding food safely

Cooked food should stay hot (140°F or above) or be cooled and refrigerated. Food-safety guidance is not to leave perishable food out more than 2 hours, or 1 hour when it's above 90°F outside.

  • Move finished food to a foil pan, cover with foil, and set it at the cool end of the grill or in an insulated cooler (no ice) lined with towels.
  • Use separate platters and tongs for raw and cooked food.
  • Keep raw meat in the cooler until its wave.

Use the griddle to double capacity

A Slot Grill griddle (plancha) turns part of the top into a flat surface that holds chopped or small food that would fall through a grate: onions, peppers, shrimp, sliced steak, eggs. It also keeps cooked items warm when set over the cool side.

Running two grills

For bigger groups, two Slot Grills side by side give you a dedicated sear grill and a dedicated hold-and-griddle grill. Owners who cook for families and groups often pair the Compact with the Full Size, which has a 20.5" × 11.25" (231 sq in) cooking area. Both flat-pack.

Crowd packing list

  • Extra charcoal (more than you think) and a second chimney
  • Two pairs of tongs (raw and cooked), a spatula, heat-safe gloves
  • Instant-read thermometer
  • Foil pans and foil; insulated cooler for hot holding
  • Separate cooler with ice for raw meat
  • Paper towels, trash bags, hand-washing water and soap
  • Metal ash can with lid

Safe internal temperatures

Guessing is how small-grill cooks go wrong, because everything happens fast over a close fire. Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from bone. USDA minimum internal temperatures:

FoodMinimum internal temp
Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts145°F, then rest 3 minutes
Ground beef, pork, veal and lamb160°F
Poultry (whole, parts, ground)165°F
Fish and shellfish145°F, or flesh opaque and separates easily with a fork
Egg dishes160°F; cook eggs until yolk and white are firm
Leftovers and casseroles165°F

Quick-start checklist

  • Grill seasoned and dry; old ash emptied
  • Stable, level, non-combustible spot with clearance
  • Charcoal lit in a chimney, ashed over
  • Two-zone fire built, grates preheated and oiled
  • Thermometer, tongs, spatula and gloves within reach
  • Clean tray ready for cooked food

Common small-grill mistakes (and the fix)

  1. Cooking before the coals are ready. Black-topped coals give smoky, weak heat and sticking. Wait until they glow under a light coat of gray ash.
  2. Wall-to-wall coals. With no cool zone, every flare-up becomes a burn. Leave at least a third of the firebox thin or empty.
  3. Crowding the grate. 146 sq in fills fast. Leave space between pieces so you can move them, and cook in waves.
  4. Moving food too early. Let it sear and release on its own; it sticks until a crust forms.
  5. Cold, dirty grates. Preheat for a few minutes, scrape, and wipe with an oiled paper towel held in tongs.
  6. Wet food. Pat meat and vegetables dry before oiling and seasoning. Moisture steams instead of searing.
  7. Judging doneness by time or color. Small fires vary. Use a thermometer.
  8. Leaving ash in the firebox. Old ash chokes airflow. Empty it once cold before the next cook.
  9. Putting the grill away wet. Raw carbon steel needs to be dried and lightly oiled. See rust prevention.

After the cook: burn-down and pack-down

  1. Let the remaining coals burn down. Don't leave a fire unattended.
  2. When ash and steel are completely cold, dump the ash into a metal container with a lid, or dispose of it where the venue or campground allows.
  3. Scrape the grates, then wipe the cooking surfaces with a light coat of oil or Slot Grill Seasoning Balm.
  4. Lift off the grates, slide out the V side panels, pull the end pieces and stack. The Compact packs to about 1" thick.
  5. Store it dry. A bag or old box keeps soot off the rest of your gear.

Safety basics (every cook)

  • Outdoors only. Never use a charcoal grill indoors, in a garage, tent, camper, boat cabin or vehicle. Burning charcoal makes carbon monoxide, which you cannot see or smell.
  • Stable, level, non-combustible surface. Bare soil, gravel, sand, concrete, pavers or a fire-safe pad. The Compact's 2⅞" air gap between the table and the bottom of the coal bed helps minimize radiant heat to the surface below, but it is still a live fire.
  • Clearance. Keep it away from tents, vehicles, siding, overhangs, dry grass and anything that can burn. Keep kids and pets back.
  • Follow local rules. Burn bans, campground, park, venue and HOA rules come first.
  • Water or a fire extinguisher nearby, plus heat-safe gloves for handling grates and panels.
  • Ash stays hot for a long time. Put it in a metal container with a lid and never pack hot steel or ash into a vehicle.

Frequently asked questions

How many people can the Slot Grill Compact feed?

It's sized for one to three people per batch, but by cooking in waves and holding food safely you can feed a larger group. For big groups, a second Slot Grill helps.

How do I keep food warm while I cook the next batch?

Cover finished food in a foil pan at the cool end of the grill or in an insulated cooler with no ice. Keep hot food at 140°F or above.

How do I keep the fire going for a long group cook?

Start with a full chimney, light a second chimney partway through, and add lit coals to the hot side as the first batch fades.

What's the best food to grill for a crowd on a small grill?

Foods that hold well (sausages, chicken thighs, wings) and foods that cook fast or stretch far (smash burgers, shrimp, fajitas).
